About

Research grounded in EMS practice

I am a paramedic and health services researcher focused on the systems that determine whether communities can access reliable emergency medical care. I currently serve as an EMS Research Fellow at the National Registry of Emergency Medical Technicians while pursuing a PhD in Health Services Management and Policy at The Ohio State University College of Public Health.

My work is informed by more than a decade of clinical experience, including 9-1-1 EMS and five years in a tertiary emergency department. That field perspective shapes a research agenda centered on practical questions of workforce sustainability, organizational performance, education, access, and policy.

Research

Areas of focus

I study EMS as a health system, with attention to the organizational and policy conditions that shape care before a patient reaches the hospital.

01

EMS workforce

Clinician retention, turnover, job satisfaction, work patterns, organizational culture, and the conditions that make EMS careers sustainable.

02

Financing and policy

Reimbursement, revenue capture, payment design, and policy mechanisms that influence EMS system capacity and service availability.

03

Education and access

Geographic access to EMS education, program performance, training pathways, and structural differences in educational opportunity.

04

Systems and operations

National EMS utilization, service delivery, behavioral health demand, operational variation, and system level performance.

Methods

Quantitative, spatial, and qualitative approaches

My work uses national EMS datasets, longitudinal and clustered regression methods, causal inference approaches, GIS and road network analysis, survey research, and constructivist grounded theory.
